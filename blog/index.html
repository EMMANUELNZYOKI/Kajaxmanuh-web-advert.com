Website planning01

What should a business website do before it tries to look impressive?

A practical note from KajaxCodeLab

A useful website begins with a job. It might help a new customer understand what you offer, make it easier to compare services, answer common questions or start a conversation. Decide which job matters most before choosing page effects or writing a long list of features.

Make the next step easy to find

Organise the page around the questions a visitor is likely to ask: who you help, what you do, why it is relevant and how to reach you. Use simple navigation and one clear call to action on each important page. On a phone, make sure the text is readable and the buttons are easy to tap.

Check the experience, not just the screenshot

Test the site on different screen sizes. Confirm that contact links work, images load efficiently and headings explain the page. After launch, observe the questions customers still ask and update the content accordingly. A business website is a working tool; its usefulness matters more than decoration alone.

Business software02

When does a spreadsheet stop being the right business tool?

A practical note from KajaxCodeLab

A spreadsheet can be an excellent starting point. It is flexible, familiar and inexpensive. The question is not whether spreadsheets are good or bad; it is whether the current way of working still fits the number of people, records and handoffs involved.

Look for recurring friction

Notice repeated copying between files, uncertainty about which version is current, manual reminders that are easy to miss, or reports that take too long to assemble. These can be signs to review the process. First map the steps and who needs each piece of information. Sometimes a clearer template or a standard product is enough.

Choose a build only when it earns its place

A custom system may make sense when the workflow is important, the handoffs are frequent and available tools cannot support the requirements. Compare the cost of building and maintaining it with the time and errors it is meant to reduce. A small, well-defined first release is usually easier to evaluate than a large wish list.

E-commerce03

A practical checklist before opening an online store

A practical note from KajaxCodeLab

Before an online store goes live, check whether a first-time visitor can understand the offer and place an order without needing help. Product names, clear photos, accurate descriptions, pricing and availability make a real difference to the buying experience.

Walk through the customer journey

Try the store on a phone, find a product, review delivery or collection information, and complete a test order. Make contact details visible. Explain what happens after an order is placed and how a customer can ask a question. Check that product categories and search help people find items quickly.

Plan the operation behind the checkout

An order is not complete when the payment screen works. Decide how stock will be updated, who prepares orders, how customers receive confirmations and how returns or support requests will be handled. A simple, dependable first version is a better place to begin than a complex store with unclear processes.
